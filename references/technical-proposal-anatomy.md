# Technical Proposal Anatomy

## Principle

A Saudi technical proposal should make three things easy to believe:

1. the bidder understands the real situation
2. the solution is executable and governed
3. risk is visible and controlled

The exact section count depends on the bid. A 15-slide event proposal and a 60-page managed-service RFP should not share the same volume, but they can share the same logic.

## Executive summary

The executive summary is not a company intro. It should compress:

- what the buyer is trying to achieve
- what makes the job operationally difficult or important
- the proposed response model
- the most decision-relevant deliverables
- how quality and governance are controlled
- the most important assumptions or inputs still required
- why the bidder is credible, only with verified proof

Avoid invented impact numbers. If there is no baseline, describe the measurement system rather than promising a percentage improvement.

## Understanding and context

Show evidence that the bid was read, not copied:

- buyer context from supplied sources
- trigger for the project
- audience or stakeholder reality
- constraints
- success definition
- sensitivity or reputation implications

Do not paraphrase every line of the RFP. Synthesize the implications.

## Objectives and measures

Separate:

- business or institutional outcomes
- communication outcomes
- operational service levels
- production quality measures
- reporting and governance measures

A KPI is credible when it has a definition, source, cadence, owner, and target basis. If the target is not supplied or benchmarked responsibly, leave it as a measurement field rather than inventing it.

## Workstreams

For each workstream define:

- purpose
- activities
- inputs
- deliverables
- frequency or quantity
- acceptance criteria
- owner
- dependencies
- relevant KPI
- price mapping ID

This schema creates the bridge to the financial model.

## Methodology

Do not write generic phases such as Discover > Design > Deliver unless each phase changes something observable.

A useful methodology explains:

- decision taken at each stage
- evidence or input used
- who approves
- artifact produced
- quality gate
- handoff to the next stage

Typical patterns:

- communication retainer: baseline > strategy > calendar > production > publishing > monitoring > monthly learning loop
- event: brief lock > concept > venue and supplier lock > production > rehearsal > live operations > closure and report
- crisis: preparedness > listening > triage > escalation > response > monitoring > recovery > learning
- digital/AI: discovery > data and security design > prototype > validation > implementation > monitoring > handover

## Deliverables and acceptance

A deliverable is not "content support". It is something countable, reviewable, or contractible.

Examples of better specification:

- monthly Arabic and English content calendar, approved before the agreed monthly cut-off
- 12 executive media reports per year with source coverage, sentiment methodology, risks, and recommended actions
- event run of show with roles, timings, cue owners, technical dependencies, and fallback procedures
- AI assistant pilot with approved knowledge sources, escalation behavior, privacy controls, acceptance scenarios, and admin documentation

Acceptance criteria should focus on objective conditions wherever possible.

## Operating model and governance

Define:

- single point of contact
- decision owners
- approval SLA assumptions
- meeting cadence
- status reporting
- issue escalation
- change request path
- urgent-request path
- supplier coordination where relevant

For multi-entity Saudi work, make the approval topology visible. A content room, campaign war room, or event command structure can be more valuable than another strategy slide.

## Schedule

Use actual dates when the RFP gives them. Otherwise show duration and dependencies.

Never show a schedule that ignores:

- client approval time
- vendor lead time
- production logistics
- permits
- access passes
- data access
- translation
- procurement or purchase orders

## Quality

Quality gates can include:

- factual verification
- Arabic language review
- brand compliance
- rights and licensing
- file specifications
- platform formatting
- accessibility where relevant
- data handling
- final approval record
- archive and source-file handover

## Risk and Plan B

A risk slide is useful only if the mitigation changes execution.

Example pattern:

| Risk | Trigger | Impact | Mitigation | Fallback | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| late venue confirmation | no signed venue by T-21 | layout and AV uncertainty | modular zoning and provisional supplier hold | reduced technical package | project lead |

## Team

Show roles because they prove operating capacity, not because staff photos fill a slide.

For each named role, only use a real person and approved CV evidence. If the team is not yet assigned, use role profiles and mark them `TBC`.

## Case evidence

A useful case study answers:

- comparable problem
- scale
- what the bidder actually owned
- constraints
- measurable or verifiable result
- relevance to this bid

If confidentiality prevents naming the buyer, anonymize the identity but never invent the result.

## Closing

A strong close clarifies the immediate decision or input needed. It should not repeat generic gratitude across multiple slides.
