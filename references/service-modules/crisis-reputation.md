# Crisis and Reputation Module

## Owns

- reputation risk assessment
- crisis governance and escalation
- crisis playbook/manual
- scenario planning
- holding statements and response templates
- simulation/tabletop exercises
- crisis advisory retainer
- incident response support
- recovery and learning
- interface with monitoring/intelligence

## Discovery questions

- What counts as a crisis vs routine issue?
- Who has authority to approve a response?
- What are the operating hours and escalation SLA?
- Which stakeholders must be informed?
- Which channels are monitored?
- Is legal/security/operations representation available?
- What languages are required?
- Is the scope preparedness, monitoring, incident response, or all three?
- What surge capacity is expected during an incident?
- Are onsite/war-room requirements included?

## Operating layers

1. preparedness and risk inventory
2. governance and decision rights
3. monitoring trigger interface
4. triage/severity model
5. response workflow
6. stakeholder communication
7. incident log
8. recovery and post-incident review

Monitoring detects signals. Crisis governance decides response. Keep ownership explicit.

## SLA contract

A crisis SLA must specify:
- trigger/event
- clock start
- response type
- coverage window
- escalation owner
- dependencies
- client approval availability
- backup route
- measurement evidence

Do not promise 30-minute response if the buyer's approval path can take hours without an urgent mechanism.

## Capacity and commercial model

Separate:
- preparedness project
- normal retainer
- monitoring layer
- on-call availability
- incident surge effort
- onsite war room
- third-party tools

24/7 service needs a coverage model, not one named consultant.

## Evidence and claims

Do not reuse sensitive crisis examples from prior clients. Anonymize patterns and use only authorized proof.

## Failure modes

- monitoring alert described as crisis resolution
- SLA not aligned to approvals
- 24/7 promise with business-hours staffing
- incident surge assumed free inside a small retainer
- legal advice promised by non-legal scope
- holding statements published without fact verification
- no recovery/post-incident loop
