# Buyer and Bid Strategy

Use this reference after requirements are grounded and before technical drafting.

## 1. Decision audience

Do not assume "the client" is one reader. Identify the likely evaluation roles only from the RFP, meeting evidence, or explicit context.

Common roles:
- procurement: completeness, comparability, contractual clarity
- technical evaluator: methodology, feasibility, staffing, acceptance criteria
- communications or marketing owner: relevance, creative-operational fit, responsiveness
- executive sponsor: risk, confidence, outcome, accountability
- finance: total cost, payment exposure, commercial assumptions
- IT/security/data: architecture, controls, support, data handling

If the audience is unknown, design for mixed review rather than inventing a persona.

## 2. Government vs semi-government vs private

### Government
Prioritize:
- mandatory compliance
- scoring traceability
- prescribed forms
- qualification evidence
- current procurement and local-content checks
- measurable delivery governance

Do not trade compliance for creative presentation.

### Semi-government
Expect a hybrid of formal procurement and executive quality expectations. Preserve formal controls while making the decision story clear.

### Private
The brief may be incomplete and the decision may be negotiated. Make assumptions visible, create scope options where useful, and separate confirmed requirements from proposed design choices.

## 3. Evaluation-weighted architecture

Create an Evaluation Coverage Ratio:

`coverage ratio = evidenced response points / applicable scored points`

This is not the buyer's score. It is an internal completeness measure.

For each criterion, track:
- weight
- mandatory evidence
- proposed section
- proof source
- residual weakness

Never infer a scoring formula not stated by the buyer.

## 4. Win-theme standard

A valid win theme contains:
- buyer need
- mechanism
- evidence
- consequence

Bad: "Integrated end-to-end excellence."

Better: "One approval queue connects content, production, and publishing so urgent work has one owner, one status, and one escalation path."

## 5. Evidence density

High-weight sections should contain more decision-grade evidence, not simply more words.

Decision-grade evidence can be:
- verified case result
- named governance control
- quantified deliverable
- acceptance criterion
- SLA with staffed operating model
- verified credential
- source-grounded regulatory control

## 6. Case selection

Select cases by similarity dimensions:
- service model
- operating pressure
- stakeholder complexity
- audience or channel
- geography
- duration
- scale
- regulatory sensitivity

A famous but irrelevant case is weaker than a smaller highly comparable case.

## 7. Page and attention budget

When page count is constrained:
1. mandatory forms and compliance
2. highest weighted criteria
3. operating model and deliverables
4. evidence
5. risk and governance
6. optional visual explanation

Do not let company profile pages consume the space needed to answer the RFP.

## 8. Value-add rule

A value-add is allowed only when:
- it solves an adjacent buyer problem
- ownership is defined
- cost treatment is explicit
- it does not create an unbounded promise

Label it Included, Optional, or Future Phase.
