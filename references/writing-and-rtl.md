# Writing, Structure, and RTL

## Default language behavior

Use the language requested by the buyer or user. A bilingual proposal is not two independent stories. Keep terminology, quantities, dates, deliverable IDs, and claims synchronized.

## Arabic proposal style

Prefer clear Modern Standard Arabic with Saudi institutional vocabulary when appropriate. Avoid inflated promotional language. Technical Arabic can retain common English terms when the buyer's own material uses them and the term is clearer that way.

Use consistent terminology. Pick one term for each concept, for example:

- نطاق العمل
- المخرجات
- معايير القبول
- التبعيات
- الافتراضات
- الحوكمة
- إدارة التغيير
- مؤشرات الأداء

Do not switch between near-synonyms if that makes contractual interpretation less clear.

## Proposal tone

The proposal should read like a capable delivery partner:

- specific
- calm
- evidence-backed
- operational
- clear about uncertainty
- easy to review

Avoid:

- empty superlatives
- generic "we are pleased" introductions that consume space without proving anything
- unsupported promises
- long paragraphs where a table would help evaluation
- fake precision
- copied market facts that do not change the solution

## One job per section or slide

Each page should primarily do one job:

- prove understanding
- explain a decision
- define scope
- show process
- show evidence
- show schedule
- show governance
- show price
- show risk

A slide that mixes five jobs becomes hard to evaluate.

## Tables

Use tables for:

- compliance
- deliverables
- schedule
- roles
- KPIs
- risks
- assumptions
- BOQ

Keep prose for synthesis and rationale.

## RTL

For Arabic artifacts:

- set paragraph direction and alignment natively rather than visually faking RTL
- verify numbers, English acronyms, URLs, currency, and parentheses after rendering
- test table column order, especially in PowerPoint and exported PDF
- avoid mixed-direction punctuation that makes a sentence hard to scan
- verify that Arabic fonts support ligatures and weight hierarchy

## Financial language

Distinguish:

- included
- optional
- excluded
- client supplied
- pass-through at cost
- subject to quote
- subject to availability
- pricing input required

Do not use vague notes such as "as needed" when they can create unlimited obligations.
