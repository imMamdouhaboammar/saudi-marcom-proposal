# Risk, Assumptions, Dependencies, and Governance

## Four distinct concepts

Do not collapse all uncertainty into a footnote.

- `Risk`: a future event that may occur and affect delivery
- `Assumption`: a condition used to design or price the offer before it is confirmed
- `Dependency`: an input or action required from another party
- `Exclusion`: something explicitly outside the offered scope or price

## Assumption quality

A useful assumption states:

- assumption
- why it is needed
- owner for confirmation
- confirmation deadline
- technical effect if false
- financial effect if false

Bad assumption: "Client will cooperate."

Better assumption: "The buyer provides consolidated feedback from one authorized approver within two business days of each submission. Additional review rounds or fragmented approver feedback may shift the delivery schedule and, where material, trigger change control."

## Risk register

Minimum fields:

- risk ID
- category
- cause / trigger
- likelihood
- impact
- exposure
- early-warning signal
- mitigation
- fallback / contingency
- owner
- commercial effect

Typical categories by this Skill's domain:

- approval delay
- source-data access
- venue uncertainty
- supplier availability
- content sensitivity
- rights/licensing
- talent/influencer availability
- regulatory/permit
- production logistics
- technology dependency
- internet/connectivity
- API/third-party dependency
- security/privacy
- language/translation
- seasonal capacity
- media budget volatility
- crisis escalation

## Governance

A proposal should show how decisions happen.

Common layers:

- sponsor / executive owner
- buyer project owner
- bidder project director
- day-to-day project manager
- workstream leads
- quality/review owner
- commercial/change owner
- crisis or escalation owner

Define:

- meeting cadence
- reporting cadence
- approval path
- issue response path
- urgent request path
- change request path

## Change control

Commercial scope creep often begins as an innocent request. Define what triggers a change assessment:

- quantity increase
- extra language
- faster SLA
- extra location
- additional event day
- new platform
- new deliverable type
- extra revision rounds outside agreed policy
- scope that requires a new license, supplier, or permit
- client delay that causes supplier rebooking or remobilization

The proposal does not need aggressive legal language. It needs a visible mechanism.
