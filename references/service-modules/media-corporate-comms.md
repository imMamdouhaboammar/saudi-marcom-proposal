# Media and Corporate Communications Module

## Owns

- corporate narrative and message architecture
- editorial planning and content operations
- media relations and press-office work
- executive and spokesperson communications
- institutional campaigns and publishing
- media-center operations when communications-led
- editorial governance and approvals
- media production when directly tied to communications outputs

## Discovery questions

Resolve before architecture:
- Who can speak for the buyer and who approves public language?
- Which audiences require distinct messages?
- Which channels are owned, earned, partner, or paid?
- Are Arabic and English equal production languages or is one source language?
- What turnaround is required for normal, urgent, and sensitive content?
- Does the scope include media outreach, guaranteed placement, or only coordination?
- Are press lists supplied, built, or licensed?
- What event/live coverage windows exist?
- What archive, rights, open-file, or raw-footage handover is required?

## Operating model

Typical layers:
1. narrative and message governance
2. editorial calendar and requests
3. content creation
4. review and approval
5. publishing/distribution coordination
6. live/urgent path
7. reporting and archive

For high-volume institutional work, use a content-room model with:
- request intake
- editorial triage
- language QA
- creative/production queue
- approval queue
- publication status
- version history
- urgent escalation lane

## Deliverable units

Possible units:
- message house
- editorial calendar
- press release
- article/op-ed
- speech/talking points
- social post/copy set
- media briefing
- interview coordination
- press kit
- media list
- monthly communications report
- coverage shift
- photo/video production day
- finished video
- executive profile asset

Each unit needs acceptance criteria. "Media relations support" alone is not a deliverable.

## Acceptance evidence

Examples:
- approved final copy
- publication-ready bilingual files
- media outreach log
- approved press kit
- publish/archive link
- version and approval record
- final media coverage report

Do not promise earned-media placement unless the commercial and media arrangement genuinely guarantees it.

## Capacity and pricing drivers

- monthly content volume
- language count
- editorial complexity
- seniority of spokesperson/executive support
- approval cycles
- urgency
- coverage hours
- production days
- travel/location
- raw/open-file handover
- licensed media database/tool costs

## Governance

Define:
- client editorial owner
- agency account/project owner
- final language approver
- sensitive-content escalation
- spokesperson approval
- normal and urgent approval SLA

## Saudi-specific relevance checks

Activate regulatory research only when the activity actually requires it:
- publishing/media activity licensing
- advertising/influencer content
- personal data in media lists or registration
- event/filming permissions
- public-sector submission requirements

Use the current General Authority for Media Regulation identity and services from official sources. Do not reuse legacy authority names.

## Common failure modes

- generic calendar with no audience/message logic
- copying the buyer's RFP language as "strategy"
- impossible content volume for staffing
- translation treated as final formatting
- promised press coverage with no distribution basis
- live coverage with no approval path
- raw-footage/IP obligations absent from pricing
- executive content without senior review capacity
