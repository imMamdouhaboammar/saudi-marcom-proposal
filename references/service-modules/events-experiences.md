# Events and Experiences Module

## Owns

- event concept and experience architecture
- guest journey
- program/run of show
- venue zoning and production coordination
- stage/AV/lighting supplier scope
- entertainment and facilitation
- registration/hospitality when requested
- staffing and onsite operations
- event-specific content interfaces
- contingency and show-calling logic

## Discovery questions

- What event type is this: corporate gathering, conference, exhibition, launch, entertainment event, ceremony, activation?
- Date, city, venue status, guest count, audience profile?
- Indoor/outdoor and venue restrictions?
- Who owns the MC, speakers, performers, and official protocol?
- Dress-code/cultural/accessibility constraints?
- What production infrastructure exists?
- What is the content and photography permission model?
- What permits or supplier accreditations may apply?
- What hospitality/catering is required?
- What fallback is needed for internet, AV, performer, weather, or schedule failure?

## Architecture

Typical layers:
1. experience intent
2. guest journey
3. program and content
4. venue and production
5. hospitality
6. staffing and governance
7. supplier management
8. rehearsal
9. live operations
10. close-out and handover

The event should be designed from attendee flow and operational reality, not a catalog of activations.

## Acceptance and readiness

Before event day verify:
- venue access
- approved floor plan
- technical rider
- cue/run sheet
- supplier confirmations
- permits/approvals when applicable
- content approval
- guest/registration data controls
- emergency contacts
- redundancy for critical systems

## Commercial drivers

- guest count
- venue and city
- load-in/load-out hours
- event days and rehearsal
- stage dimensions
- AV specification
- entertainment talent
- hospitality
- staffing shifts
- supplier cancellation terms
- travel/accommodation
- permits
- internet/redundancy
- custom fabrication

## Saudi regulatory gate

Classify the activity before selecting a GEA service. Entertainment Event Permit and Entertainment Show Permit can have different requirements and lead times.

Do not write "GEA permit required" as a universal statement for every corporate event. Verify current applicability.

## Failure modes

- concept impossible in the venue
- activity inappropriate for dress code/audience
- permit lead time discovered after schedule commitment
- single internet connection is a critical dependency
- catering and program compete for same time/space
- supplier quote expires before award
- event day staffing not aligned to operating hours
- entertainment content boundaries not agreed
- production scope priced without rehearsal/load-in
